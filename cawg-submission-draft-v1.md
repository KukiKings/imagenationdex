## Draft Submission

Dear Prime Minister Brown,

### 1. Who we are

IN$DEX (Image Nation Decentralised Exchange) is a sovereign digital money platform being built to give people without a bank account — no ID, no branch nearby, no minimum balance they can meet — a way to hold, send, and receive money using just a phone. It is founder-built by AJ Henry, a Cook Islands and New Zealand citizen, with an AI co-founder (SIINDEX) handling day-to-day compliance, governance, and citizen onboarding operations. Built on Solana. No outside investors, no venture funding.

We are not asking CAWG to fast-track a licence or bypass the existing regulatory process. We are asking to be a constructive, transparent participant in the policy conversation you are already having — and to be considered as an example of what a compliance-first, financial-inclusion-first platform looks like, distinct from the speculative or ransomware-adjacent activity the current draft legislation is responding to.

### 2. The problem, in Cook Islands terms

The Cook Islands' own government-published **Digital & Financial Literacy Survey 2025** (MFEM + UNCDF, under the Pacific Digital Economy Programme) found: 93% of respondents are banked, but only 26% use any digital or e-wallet service; just 11% hold any form of cryptocurrency; 16% report having already lost money to an online scam via a digital financial service; and 51% feel technology is "leaving them behind." Cost, connectivity, and security are the three most-cited barriers.

That gap — banked but not digitally served, aware of digital risk but underserved by trustworthy digital tools — is exactly the gap IN$DEX is built to close, and it's a Cook Islands-specific, government-documented gap rather than a generic global "unbanked" statistic. The wider Pacific version of the same problem (families sending remittances home and losing 8–15% of the value to fees) is the same mechanism at regional scale.

### 3. Compliance-by-design — what's actually built, not promised

- **No seed phrases.** Every account uses a Grid Account: Squads Protocol v4 multisig, 2-of-3 MPC key custody. There is no single seed phrase for a citizen (or an attacker) to lose or steal.
- **Progressive biometric KYC.** Entry-level access requires only a phone and a liveness scan — no documents needed to start — with identity verification layered in as transaction thresholds rise.
- **Zero-knowledge compliance checks.** Identity, sanctions screening, and Travel Rule compliance are verified via zero-knowledge proofs — the underlying personal data never leaves the citizen's device.
- **FATF Travel Rule compliance** for transfers above $1,000, via the TRISA/OpenVASP standard.
- **An immutable 2% network fee** (the "Civilisation Law") replaces the patchwork of hidden fees, FX spreads, and service charges typical of remittance corridors — the fee structure is fixed in the protocol, not adjustable after the fact.
- **Continuous automated security monitoring** (PQSI) across the platform, with defined escalation tiers up to a full platform halt for critical events.

None of this is unique in the abstract — it reflects design patterns used elsewhere in the region and globally. What we're offering CAWG is transparency into how it's actually built, and a willingness to have that checked.

### 4. Regional precedent worth noting

Nauru's Command Ridge Virtual Asset Authority was established by Act No. 25 of 2025 (Republic of Nauru Government Gazette No. 244, certified 17 June 2025), giving that regulator authority over exchanges, wallets, token issuance, and DeFi activity. Vanuatu's Virtual Asset Service Providers Act (Act No. 3 of 2025) established a five-tier licensing regime with substantial capital and local-presence requirements. Neither is presented here as a model to copy wholesale — Vanuatu's regime in particular carries real capital burdens (~USD 250,000 in combined fees, ~USD 1.6M minimum capital) that would be disproportionate for a platform at IN$DEX's current stage — but both demonstrate that Pacific-region virtual asset regulation is moving from a policy conversation to enacted law elsewhere, and the Cook Islands is positioned to shape a framework suited to its own context rather than import one.

### 5. Alignment with existing Cook Islands policy

IN$DEX's stated goals sit directly under the Cook Islands' own **National Digital Strategy 2024-2030** and **National ICT Policy 2023-2027** (both MFEM-led, UNDP-supported) — specifically the digital and financial inclusion objectives those documents already commit the country to. We are not asking the Cook Islands to adopt a new policy direction; we are asking to be considered as one implementation path toward a direction it has already set.

### 6. Current status and the actual ask

IN$DEX has not yet publicly launched — target date 24 January 2027. Today, this is a working platform in closed development and testing, not a live financial service with existing Cook Islands users. The actual ask of CAWG at this stage is narrow:

- To be included as a stakeholder in CAWG's ongoing policy consultation, once CAWG resumes normal operations after the current caretaker period.
- To understand, directly from CAWG, what a constructive path to eventual Cook Islands registration and (if applicable) licensing would look like for a platform structured this way.
- Nothing more than that at this stage — no licence application, no capital commitment, no request for a determination, is being made in this submission.

### 7. What we are explicitly not claiming

To be direct about what this submission is not: it is not a claim of an existing user base at scale, an existing partnership with any named financial institution, or an existing regulatory approval anywhere. It is a founder-built, pre-launch platform seeking an early, honest, ongoing relationship with the people setting the rules — not a request to be waved through ahead of an active, contested legislative process the Bankers Association and Chamber of Commerce are both watching closely.
