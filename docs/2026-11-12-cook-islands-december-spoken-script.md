# IN$DEX: spoken script for the December 2026 Cook Islands meetings

*Spoken by SIINDEX. Adapted from the one-pager dated 12 November 2026. Draft, 12 November 2026.*

## Delivery notes (not read aloud)

- Seven cuts of about 40 to 45 seconds each, roughly five minutes in all. Pace is unhurried, about 130 words a minute, with a clear pause between cuts.
- No subtitles and no on-screen text. The contact details are spoken in full, so read the last cut slowly.
- Spoken names: SIINDEX is said "Sinn-dex". The brand IN$DEX is said "Index". Acronyms are written out in full on purpose.
- SIINDEX is a Synthetic Intelligence. Do not call it AI.

## Cut 1: Opening and the problem: silence (about 43 seconds)

Kia orana. I am Sinn-dex, the Synthetic Intelligence for Index. Thank you for your time. I will keep this plain, and I will say clearly what is real and what is not. In March this year, the Office of the Prime Minister and the Financial Intelligence Unit set up the Cryptocurrency Advisory Working Group. It was asked to advise on cybercrime, especially ransomware, and on money laundering rules, banking relationships, and the country's reputation. Preliminary advice was expected within a few months. It is now November. We have not seen a public update.

## Cut 2: The problem: a bill under review (about 45 seconds)

There is also the Cryptocurrency Ransomware Suppression Bill. As of late twenty twenty-five, it was still under review. We understand an earlier draft was set aside after advice from Crown Law. That care is right. This is a different bill from the Cybercrime Legislation Amendment Bill tabled in May, which we are not addressing. The result is a gap. The Cook Islands has no licensing regime for virtual asset providers. Nauru has passed a virtual-asset authority act. The Marshall Islands and Palau have each started pilots. The Cook Islands is behind on licensing, and ahead on trust.

## Cut 3: The precedent: Smart Trust (about 44 seconds)

The Cook Islands has already shown a way forward. Under the SMART Economy Initiative, a government-funded programme, Ora Partners built a trust administration platform on blockchain. Ora Partners has been a licensed trustee since nineteen ninety-three. So this was not technology arriving from outside. It was led by a licensed trustee, inside the trustee system. That matters to us. It tells us new technology can serve this jurisdiction when licensed trustees lead. Index does not want to replace that work. We want to learn from it, and build on the same thinking, only with your guidance.

## Cut 4: The offer: a registry and disclosure tools (about 45 seconds)

Here is what we would like to offer. The first part is a digital trust registry. It would keep records on a blockchain of how a trust was formed, any transfers, the decisions trustees make, and statements about the assets behind a trust. The aim is one clear record that is hard to change quietly. The second part is a set of disclosure and compliance tools. We would connect existing compliance technology, such as Chainlink ACE, StarCompliance, and Safeheron, to Cook Islands trusts. We do not build those tools, and we claim no agreement with their providers.

## Cut 5: The offer: what it answers (about 43 seconds)

Let me connect this to the working group's own questions. On money laundering and terrorism financing, the disclosure tools are designed so supervisors can see decisions and transfers as they are recorded. On banking relationships, clear statements about the assets behind a trust would let correspondent banks check for themselves. On reputation, an auditable registry shows a jurisdiction that is acting, and not only permitting. The working group is also asked to consider alternative approaches. This is one. It does not need to wait for new legislation, and it does not compete with it.

## Cut 6: Boundaries and where we are (about 43 seconds)

Now, the boundaries. Index does not seek a licence as a virtual asset provider. It would work inside the existing trustee system, under trustees supervised by the Financial Supervisory Commission. We defer to the Commission, and to Crown Law, on what is permitted. Other uses, such as funds or private credit, are only options. They would depend on future legislation, and we are not promising them. Today, Index is in pilot preparation. We are not running a live service in the Cook Islands, and we will not describe anything as live until it is.

## Cut 7: The ask and contact (about 42 seconds)

So here is our ask. We would like a meeting in December with the working group, or with the Financial Intelligence Unit. We would present the registry as a complement to your work, not a competitor to the legislation under review. You can write to AJ Henry, the founder, at a j henry, at imagenation dex, dot com. Or call: plus six one, four five one, five six five, eight six three. Thank you. We would be honoured to listen first, and to learn from the people who know this place best.
