## Checklist to complete registration

### 1. Registry account approval
Status: submitted, awaiting Registrar approval by email. No action pending on AJ's side until that approval arrives — confirmed process, per `cook-islands-registration-prep.md`.

### 2. Company name search / clearance
The Cook Islands registry requires the name be in English and not "the same or nearly the same" as an existing registered entity. "Image Nation DEx Limited" has been decided as the intended name, but a formal name search does not appear to have been logged as completed yet in this repo's records. **Action:** run the name search (registry's own name-search tool) before or as part of filing Form A-1, including close variants ("Image Nation DEX," "Image Nation," "ImageNation," "Index") — this was flagged in the source document as worth checking rather than assuming clear.

### 3. Resolve foreign-enterprise status
This determines the incorporation fee (NZD $75 vs. NZD $750) and whether BTIB (Business Trade and Investment Board) approval is required before registration can proceed. AJ is a Cook Islands citizen resident in Australia — whether Australian residency affects "foreign enterprise" status under the Development Investment Act is an open question, not yet resolved as of the source document's writing. **CONFIRM WITH BTIB DIRECTLY AND/OR COUNSEL** — this is explicitly flagged in the source material as something not to guess.

### 4. Registered office / physical Cook Islands address
Form A-1 requires a registered office and a principal place of business, and the registered office must be a physical Cook Islands street address (a PO Box alone is stated as insufficient). Two options noted in the source material:
- A family or personal contact's address in Rarotonga, if that person agrees to receive official documents on the company's behalf — potentially no cost.
- A licensed registered agent — standard practice for an offshore-resident owner. **CONFIRM WITH REGISTERED AGENT/COUNSEL:** whether a registered agent is legally required for this entity type, or only customary; and the actual current fee (an approximate NZD $600–700/year figure appears in prior project research but is explicitly marked there as unverified against any government source).

### 5. Beneficial ownership documentation
Company registries generally collect beneficial-ownership and identity information on directors/shareholders as part of registration and ongoing AML obligations, and a licensed registered agent (if used) would typically have its own KYC requirements on top of the registry's. This repo's own research does not confirm the specific beneficial-ownership documentation the Cook Islands registry or a registered agent would require for this particular entity type. **CONFIRM WITH REGISTERED AGENT/COUNSEL** exactly what identity/ownership documentation is required, beyond what has already been submitted for the registry account itself (a Cook Islands driver's licence, per the source document).

### 6. Complete Form A-1
Sections: Company, Directors, Shares. Not yet filed. The "primary business activity" field is flagged separately below (step 8) as carrying regulatory weight beyond a simple administrative description.

### 7. Pay the incorporation fee
NZD $75 for a Cook Islands (citizen/domestic) company, or NZD $750 for an overseas/foreign enterprise — contingent on step 3 above being resolved first.

### 8. Describe the primary business activity carefully
This field on Form A-1 has consequences beyond the form itself: describing the business as a decentralised exchange, token issuer, payments provider, or remittance service may trigger Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) licensing considerations, and no Cook Islands-specific virtual-asset/token regulatory category currently exists (the closest is a draft Cryptocurrency (Ransomware Suppression) Bill 2025, not yet enacted). **This is explicitly flagged in the source material as a question for a Cook Islands lawyer, not something to resolve unilaterally on the form** — the wording that gets the fastest incorporation is not necessarily the wording that holds up in a later FSC conversation.

### 9. Certificate of Incorporation
Issued by email once the Registrar approves the filed Form A-1 and payment. Not yet reached.

### 10. Post-incorporation: business bank account
Referenced as a planned next step in other project planning documents (`roadmap-v2.md`, `launch-runway-plan-2026.md`) but not yet started, and not itself part of the Ministry of Justice registration process. **CONFIRM WITH REGISTERED AGENT/COUNSEL and/or the chosen bank** what documentation a Cook Islands business bank account will require (likely the Certificate of Incorporation plus beneficial-ownership/KYC documentation, but this repo's research does not confirm specifics for any named bank).

### 11. Separate track: FSC / remittance or token licensing
Company registration (steps 1–9 above) is a distinct process from any licensing that may apply to actually operating a remittance platform or issuing a token under FSC's remit (see step 8, and `docs/legal/fsc-correspondence-draft.md`). Completing registration does **not** by itself clear IN$DEX to operate a licensed remittance business or issue a token. As of `BUILD_LOG.md`, no FSC correspondence has been sent and no counsel has been retained to advise on this separate track.

### 12. Retain legal counsel
Not yet done, per `BUILD_LOG.md`. Recommended before finalizing step 8 (primary business activity wording) in particular, since that field has regulatory consequences that a lawyer is better placed to weigh than a registration form alone. See `docs/legal/lawyer-engagement-letter-draft.md`.
