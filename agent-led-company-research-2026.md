## Part 1 — Fact-check of the 12 pasted additions

### ✅ CONFIRMED — Polsia's raise (Addition 7)

Real, and the details hold up. **$30M at a $250M valuation**, closed **25 May 2026**, founder **Ben Cera**. Investors: Sound Ventures, True Ventures, Offline Ventures, Adjacent, Tekton Ventures, Drysdale Ventures, Vaynerfund plus angels. The agent handled the data room, investor briefings and diligence; Cera joined only the final calls. Roughly **7,600 businesses** on the platform, approaching **$10M ARR**.

The pasted line "the founder just showed up for signatures" is a fair paraphrase of what reporting describes.

### ✅ CONFIRMED, with a material correction — Lyzr (Additions 7, 11)

Real and larger than described. **$100M Series B**, agent **SivaClaw**, **130+ investors** fielded, dozens of investment memos drafted, and the agent tracked which slides investors lingered on. The round drew **$400M in interest — four times target** — and valued Lyzr at roughly **$500M**, double its March 2026 valuation. Covered by TechCrunch and Bloomberg, 9 July 2026.

**Correction:** the pasted document says Surendira "**is seeking** $100M." The round **closed on 9 July 2026**. Writing about a closed round in the present tense is the kind of error that gets noticed by exactly the investors this positioning is meant to impress.

**Also relevant:** Lyzr ran an earlier agent, *Agent Sam*, on its Series A, cutting a month-long cycle to two weeks. So this is a repeated, refined practice at one company — stronger evidence than a single event.

### ⚠️ DIRECTIONALLY RIGHT, NUMBERS OFF — Polsia's Trustpilot rating (Addition 10)

Pasted: "1.7/5 with 40 reviews" and "reviews averaging 2.1/5."

Actual: **1.8/5 across 35 reviews, 80% one-star**, as of June 2026 — up from 20 reviews in May, with the score **falling from 2.1** as more customers reported the same failures. The document appears to have merged two snapshots taken at different times into one sentence.

The trend matters more than the number, and the trend is the document's strongest point: **the rating is deteriorating as the user base grows.**

### 🔴 MISREPRESENTED — the Gartner statistic (Addition 11)

Pasted: "Gartner reports 42% of enterprises expect to deploy AI agents in 2026, up from just 17% in 2025."

This conflates two separate Gartner findings and misstates both:

- Gartner's actual figure is **40% of enterprise *applications*** will feature task-specific AI agents by 2026, up from **less than 5%** in 2025 — that's applications, not enterprises.
- The **17%** is real but means "**organisations that have deployed AI agents to date**," not a 2025 baseline. The matching forward figure is **60%+ within two years**, not 42%.

**The serious omission:** the same Gartner research predicts **40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by 2027** — runaway costs, unclear ROI, governance failures. Related industry data: **88% of agent pilots never ship**, and only **31% of enterprises running an embedded agent have one in production**.

The pasted document cites Gartner's adoption number to argue "can you afford *not* to use one?" while silently dropping Gartner's failure number from the same body of work. That is selective citation, and if it reaches an investor deck it is the single most likely claim to be checked and to embarrass us.

### ⚠️ REAL BUT CHERRY-PICKED — market sizing (Addition 9)

The "$6.56B in 2025 → $75.14B by 2034 at 31.13% CAGR" figure is real — it is **Fortune Business Insights'** projection. But it is the most optimistic of several competing estimates:

| Firm | 2025 size | 2034 projection | CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fortune Business Insights | $6.56B | **$75.14B** | 31.13% |
| DataIntelo | $3.76B | $36.49B | 28.5% |
| Grand View Research | $5.35B | — | — |
| The Niche Research | $5.6B | $18.9B | 16.8% |

2034 estimates vary by **roughly 4×**; CAGR estimates span **16.8%–31.13%**. Quoting only the top of that range as settled fact is the same failure mode as the **elizaOS star-count inflation caught on 2026-07-24** (pasted doc claimed 18.8k stars; actual was 7,878).

**Use the range externally, and name the source.** "Between $19B and $75B by 2034 depending on methodology" is credible. "$75.14B" alone invites a correction in public.

### ⚠️ UNVERIFIED — Worknet, AutoFirm, Sam Altman, Pieter Levels (Additions 8, 9)

Not independently confirmed in this pass. The Altman "one-person billion-dollar company by 2030" remark is widely repeated but was a speculative comment, not a forecast — do not cite it as analysis. Treat all four as illustrative colour, not evidence, until checked.

## Part 2 — Additions 13–18 (new, grounded in the verification above)

### Addition 13 — Plan for the failure mode, because the same analyst predicts it

Gartner forecasts **40% of agentic AI projects cancelled by 2027**, and **88% of pilots never ship**. The pasted document's framing — "the question is no longer *can* an agent raise money, it's *can you afford not to*" — is survivorship bias. We are being shown Polsia and Lyzr because they worked.

**What IN$DEX should do:** state the failure rate in our own materials before anyone else does. A deck that says *"40% of these projects fail by 2027 — here is specifically why ours doesn't"* is far stronger than one that pretends the risk isn't there. The three named causes are runaway cost, unclear ROI and governance failure; IN$DEX has a concrete answer to the third already (PQSI threat tiers, Human Validation Zones, the Seven Security Laws) and should say so.

### Addition 14 — Polsia's real failure mode is the exact problem IN$DEX already spent months fixing

This is the most valuable finding in the whole pass, and the pasted document missed it.

Beyond the Trustpilot score, a publicly documented investigation checked three companies Polsia claimed to have launched — **FleetNova**, **DeckFlow** and **Panelwright**. Every one was a well-designed landing page **with no sign-up button, no login, no pricing page and no payment integration**. Purely cosmetic. Meanwhile the dashboard advertised "1,317 companies launched." A separate user audit reported **41 of 47 tasks marked complete**, of which 24 went out with wrong names or wrong prices, 7 errored, and 6 failed outright — a real success rate of **21.3%**.

That is not a reliability problem. **That is Kind-2 fabrication** — the precise bug class this codebase has been sweeping since 2026-07-23: screens that look finished, report success, and have nothing behind them.

**Why this matters strategically:** IN$DEX's anti-fabrication discipline is not an aspiration to put in a deck. It is *already built and already documented* — the honest-zero-state pattern, the "never invent an RPC that doesn't exist" policy, the Law-7 immutable receipts, the daily audit skill, and a `gotchas.md` recording every correction with dates. The market leader in this category is currently being publicly dismantled for doing the opposite.

**What IN$DEX should do:** make verifiability the pitch, not reliability. Anyone can claim reliable. Publish the audit trail — the corrections log, the "not live yet" labels, the deliberate refusal to fake execution. Polsia's own users are documenting the counterfactual for us.

### Addition 15 — The "zero-employee" positioning is already obsolete

The pasted document proposes positioning IN$DEX around the zero-employee model. But the same sources reporting Polsia's raise state the funds are for **growing the US sales and marketing team and hiring more engineers**.

Polsia abandoned the zero-employee model **within weeks of raising on it**.

**What IN$DEX should do:** do not build brand positioning on a claim its own exemplar has already walked away from. It is falsifiable in one search, and hostile journalists search. The durable claim is not "no employees" — it is **"SIINDEX does the operational work, and every consequential decision has a named human accountable for it."** That is true, defensible, and doesn't expire.

### Addition 16 — The regulatory exposure of agent-led fundraising, which the document does not mention at all

This is the largest risk in the pasted document and it is entirely absent.

Lyzr and Polsia are established companies in established jurisdictions raising conventional equity. **IN$DEX is not in that position:**

- The AUSTRAC VASP/RSP registration (AAN 263945366) is **submitted and under assessment — not approved**.
- The Cook Islands entity is **not yet registered**.
- The INDX mint decision is **formally paused** pending verification, reconciliation, multisig handover and legal/tax review.
- Cook Islands Parliament is **dissolved**, with an election on 12 August 2026.

Letting an SI conduct investor outreach, draft memos and answer diligence questions on behalf of an entity in that posture is materially different from what Lyzr did. Anything SIINDEX says to a prospective investor is a representation made by the business. The unsafe-claim corrections landed this session — a `$2.50 · 10.4×` price forecast in SIINDEX's own voice, "guaranteed" returns, fixed USD valuations of an unlaunched token — are exactly the statements that become actionable when made to an investor rather than a website visitor.

**What IN$DEX should do:** agent-led fundraising is **gated on legal review**, not on build capability. Building it is fine. Pointing it at real investors before the entity and registrations are real is not. This belongs in the Human Validation Zone list alongside Solana transactions and 98/2 changes.

### Addition 17 — Both proven cases kept a human on final terms. That's the finding, not the compromise.

Worth stating plainly because the pasted document treats the hybrid model as a cautious middle path. It isn't. It is **what actually happened in both verified cases**:

- Polsia: the agent ran the data room, briefings and diligence. **Cera took the final calls.**
- Lyzr: SivaClaw handled the entire top of funnel. **Humans stepped in for final terms and commitments.**

There is no verified case of an agent closing a round unsupervised. The hybrid is not the conservative option — it is the only option with evidence behind it. IN$DEX should adopt it because it is proven, and say so in those terms.

### Addition 18 — Publish a corrections log as a product surface

Follows directly from 13, 14 and the market-sizing problem.

Polsia's collapse in trust is a **transparency** failure more than a technical one: tasks marked complete that weren't, credits charged for failed actions, no clean data export, support escalations unanswered for weeks. Users had no way to see what was really happening until they'd already lost money.

IN$DEX already produces the raw material for the opposite: `gotchas.md` is a dated, public-ready record of every error found and fixed, including the ones that were embarrassing — a live time bomb in the wallet adapter, a `$0.35` price contradicting canon in the security document, fabricated data across ~50 screens, and a stale git lock that silently blocked every commit for two days.

**What IN$DEX should do:** ship a public "What We Got Wrong" page generated from that log. No competitor in this category can copy it, because copying it requires having actually kept the record. It converts three months of unglamorous correction work into the most credible trust asset the platform has — and it is the natural companion to the Live Public Dashboard already in the list as Addition 2.
